# Cost analysis

Use the user's annual distance, ownership period and fuel assumptions; state the horizon, the distance and the units once. Separate purchase, fuel, insurance, tax, maintenance, repairs and expected resale value. Date every price and rate and cite current local sources. Show the assumptions and a range for uncertain costs; a calculation is not an insurance or tax quote.

- Ownership cost = purchase − expected resale + operating costs over the horizon. NEVER add depreciation as well — ALWAYS treat purchase − resale as the depreciation, on every page of one comparison.
- Fuel or energy = distance ÷ 100 × consumption per 100 km × unit price.
- ALWAYS compute totals, differences and rankings by running code over one input set saved in the research record (`../SKILL.md`), and copy every repeated figure from that output. NEVER a total from mental arithmetic. Cost: 5.5 L/100 km × 125,000 km × 36 Kč/L published as 105,000 Kč instead of 247,500 Kč, and the page's stated gap between two cars matched none of its own totals.
- ALWAYS check from the official rule that the tax covers the vehicle's category in that jurisdiction and year before adding a tax line. NEVER carry a rate over from an older comparison or another category. Cost: a comparison charged Czech road tax on passenger cars, which the tax does not cover in 2025 and 2026 (https://app.fs.gov.cz/dotazy-verejnosti/dotaz/v-roce-2022-byl-zakon-c-161993-sb-o-dani-silnicni-ve-zneni-pozdejsich-predpisu-novelizovan-jak-se-touto-novelou-zmenilo-vymezeni-predmetu-dane-silnicni-1816).
- After an input changes, rerun the calculation and update every page that shows a result.
